# trusted-firmware — specification

> **Status: NORMATIVE** · Scope: trusted-firmware · Last verified: 2026-10-17
> Conflicts with this document are defects. Fix them here first.

## Purpose

trusted-firmware is not a layer of the Go stack and embeds no Go layer: it is a device repository under the owner's HSM and device split, the device tier.
It owns the device firmware, the secure world and on-device key handling, and is the firmware framework and SDK for every device application: bare-metal ANSI C, AVR first, with architecture ports behind the HAL and boards as data; applications live in their own `trusted-firmware-<application>` repositories and inherit the trusted computing base, attestation, onboarding, signed OTA and the secure bootloader, and the firmware itself carries no application logic.
`sdk/go` is the Layer 0 Go SDK that drives it, with zero in-house dependencies and nothing below it to inherit or re-export; go-xkms and go-trusted-platform import it.
It provides the go-xkms backend adapter for its devices, one of the hardware crypto implementations go-quicbaas passes to go-xkms.
Its devices consume go-xkms's generic DICE and attestation verification through the adapter seam, and are provisioned and attested by go-trusted-platform.

## Boundaries

- Owns the AVR wire protocol and the firmware that speaks it.
- Owns `sdk/go` (host-sdk): Layer 0, zero in-house dependencies, no private keys; consumed by go-trusted-platform at `v0.3.0-alpha`.
- Owns the `xkms` adapter module (xkms-adapter, `github.com/jeremyhahn/trusted-firmware/xkms`) and all AVR-specific xkms code: one of the hardware crypto implementations go-quicbaas passes to go-xkms, registered through go-xkms's backend registry hook and consumed by go-quicbaas's composition; go-trusted-platform and xkey reach the devices through go-quicbaas and never import the adapter.
- Embeds no Go layer: the firmware and `sdk/go` import no project of the stack, and the `xkms` adapter module uses go-xkms only for its backend interfaces and registry hook, adding a backend and never re-exporting or re-modelling go-xkms services.
- Brings no database, cluster, engine or store of its own: device state lives on the device, and records about devices live in the deployment's one datastore, owned by the layers above.
- go-xkms holds generic DICE, EAT, device-attest-01, the verifiers, the backend interfaces and the registration hook, and nothing AVR-specific.
- go-quicbaas has a generic verifier and no firmware dependency.
- Never owns DICE ASN.1, certificate profiles, DICE verification or attestation verification: go-xkms owns them.
- Never owns device enrollment, the device registry or fleet policy: go-trusted-platform owns them.
- A gap in go-xkms or go-trusted-platform is filed against that repo, never worked around here.
- Never ports another platform before AVR has every primitive and a proven physical end-to-end run (#7); design in `docs/multi-arch/architecture.md`. The order once AVR is proven on the Nano and the Mega (#7): the SE052F, then the i.MX 95's Cortex-M7 as the cortex-m reference part, then the STM32H5 with the SE052F on its bus as the reference trusted device; the OPTIGA Trust M, RP2350, ESP32-P4 and nRF54L15 come after the SE052F and the STM32H5, with no order of work among the RP2350, ESP32-P4 and nRF54L15 yet, because the owner decides when AVR is proven. Two exceptions, by the owner's rulings of 2026-10-18 (CA m623): the ESP32-P4 (`esp32-p4`, #48), and the cortex-m ADRs (#14, #15) with the generic port on QEMU mps2-an505 (#161).
- Never decodes a sensor: the device offers capability, the host supplies meaning.
- Never described as "TCG DICE compliant": several DICE hardware requirements are met only procedurally on a classic AVR.

| ota | in progress | Signed over-the-air updates of the whole image, firmware and application together, on every board with a boot section, over the network (TCP through the W5100 or W5500) and over serial. tfw-boot verifies the row key's `OTA_ACTIVATE` authorization at reset (#137), and on the ATmega2560 `OTA_CONFIRM`'s too, from the confirm request at EEPROM `0x0EA` (#231). Each image carries one update path, selected at build time by its object list, never by `#if` (owner ruling 2026-10-17, item 20). The ATtiny85, with no boot section, has no OTA (decided under ruling 109, #52 comment 152690, 2026-10-26). A new image boots once on trial within a per-board timeout; the control plane confirms it after attestation, a failed trial rolls back by itself, and recovery is another OTA round. An anti-rollback floor, advanced at confirm, stops any update from lowering the security version. A new status code answers a command in the wrong OTA state. Decision 8 (owner ruling 2026-10-17, items 21 and 55): `OTA_INFO`, `OTA_ACTIVATE` and `OTA_CONFIRM` take command ids 0x6C, 0x6D and 0x6E, and a block that cannot be verified fails closed. `OTA_ACTIVATE` and `OTA_CONFIRM` are authorized by the row key (CA m502 item 2, 2026-10-17, option (a) of #137): each needs a COSE_Sign1 ES256 signature (RFC 9052 §4.2 and §4.4, RFC 9053 §2.1), by the board id's image key, over a detached payload that names the action, the image's measurement and its version; tfw-boot checks it at the next reset with the key that verifies the image, one gate on every tier, with no session, no nonce and no TRNG; the version against the anti-rollback floor stops replay; the element tier keeps its operator session and adds the check; the signature arrives in a new `OTA_AUTH` (0x6F) before the command, and a command without one answers `TFW_ERR_AUTH_REQUIRED` (17) (`docs/ota/api.md`, `docs/ota/architecture.md`). Owner ruling 2026-10-17 (owner, CA m499), items 72 to 75: open question 10 is closed by item 56, so the failed image is never copied back in and the failed measurement is not kept in the platform block (`docs/ota/adr-ota-image-format-and-trust.md:687-690`, `docs/ota/architecture.md:123,307,460,720`); the boot hash fold is option (c), the extra store read stays and a staged boot costs about one more image hash on the element, and the fold slice is closed unmerged, because options (a) and (b) would have changed the STAGED resume semantics (`docs/ota/architecture.md:418,424`); the SUIT-subset manifest (#78) travels with the image in the trailer, v1 plus `board_id` with no trailer v2, `tfw-boot` verifies it before activation on every board, and the 4 KB parts (ATmega328P, ATmega32U4) do trailer-only verification (`docs/ota/adr-ota-image-format-and-trust.md:156-159`). The EEPROM 0x100 change is a follow-up after OTA (#125). Seam to go-quicbaas: its reference-values store holds each image, its SUIT envelope and its CoRIM. Seam to go-xkms: its provisioning seam row, which go-xkms owns, places the verification key in the element's slot; this SPEC mirrors that row once it lands on go-xkms's main. What runs today: tfw-boot's trailer gate and its one trial boot, with the 8 s watchdog armed before the trial jump and, in the application, re-armed, petted and the per-board deadline counted. The build seals an AVR image for its board: `make image-tbs` emits trailer v1's signed span with the board id for go-xkms to sign, and `make image-seal` wraps the signature into the stream `tfwctl ota-stage` sends and the region tfw-boot's session writes (`tools/image-seal/`, `docs/ota/user.md`). `OTA_INFO` (0x6C), `OTA_ACTIVATE` (0x6D) and `OTA_CONFIRM` (0x6E) are on the AVR wire, serial only, `OTA_ACTIVATE` and `OTA_CONFIRM` each behind `OTA_AUTH`: `OTA_CONFIRM` in `TRIAL` writes a confirm request bound to the running image's measurement and leaves the deadline running, and on the 2560 tfw-boot confirms from that request at the next reset and advances the floor, while a trial reset without one rolls back. `tfwsim` runs all three from `sdk/go` and `tfwctl`, with each wrong-state refusal and `TFW_ERR_AUTH_REQUIRED` without `OTA_AUTH` (`sdk/go/cmd/tfwctl/e2e_ota_test.go`), and given `--boot-key` the software-tier `tfwsim` runs the real tfw-boot at power on and at every reset, so the round over its pty boots an activated image on trial, ends an unconfirmed one in recovery, leaves an image below the floor untaken with its `MARK` kept and forgets the `MARK` of an activation signed for another measurement (#52, `sdk/go/cmd/tfwctl/e2e_ota_round_test.go`).
